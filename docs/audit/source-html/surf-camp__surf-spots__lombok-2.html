Sumbawa Surf Spots

The best Sumbawa surf spots you can plan your trip with confidence

Sumbawa rewards the surfers who make the effort to get here.

Why Choose Sumbawa for Surfin

The waves stay wonderfully uncrowded, paddle out at the right time and you might share a world-class break with only a handful of others, or even have it to yourself.

The island is split into two main surf zones: West Sumbawa, home to heavy hitters like Scar Reef, Supersuck, and Yoyos, and East Sumbawa, ruled by the legendary A-frame at Lakey Peak. Between them you’ll find every kind of wave, from sharp, mechanical barrels to gentle, forgiving reef breaks. Add warm water year-round, affordable food and stays, and that unmistakable “islands as they used to be” feeling, and it’s easy to see why Sumbawa has become a bucket-list surf trip.

We'll walk you through the simple prep, the top spots, and how the levels stack up.

Feel the Challenge of Sumbawa's Waves

Top Surf Spots

Surf Spot Level

Many of the marquee breaks fire over shallow, live coral reef, with real power and current when the swell is up. That’s exactly what draws experienced surfers here, also means respect, preparation, and honest self-assessment matter more than at your average beach break.

Here's the practical stuff that makes a Sumbawa surf trip smooth, safe, and stress-free.

Simple Tips

Sumbawa is remote, and surf shops are few and far between, so come self-sufficient. Bring the right boards for reef breaks (and a spare if you can), plus backup leashes, fins, and plenty of wax. Reef booties are a must for shallow, low-tide sessions, and a rash guard, reef-safe sunscreen, and a basic first-aid kit for coral scrapes are all wise additions. Since the waves pack a punch, pack for power rather than for gentle beach days.

The dry season, roughly May to September (with April and October as solid shoulder months), is prime time. This is when consistent southwest swells and offshore trade winds groom the reefs into their best form, with June and July often the most reliable for size. If you’re a beginner or intermediate, the wet season (November to March) can actually be lovely with smaller, mellower waves, lighter winds, glassy mornings, and even thinner crowds. Whatever the season, dawn patrol usually serves up the cleanest conditions before the wind picks up.

Top Surf Spots

Here are the breaks that put Sumbawa on the map.

Western Spots

Northern Rights

A faster right-hand reef break in the Kertasari area, just a short scooter ride from where we’re based in Moro Bay. It works best on mid to high tide with a clean swell and suits intermediate to advanced surfers. Mind the rocks in the line-up, and you’ll be rewarded with punchy, uncrowded rights.

Surf spot level:

  • Intermediate
  • Advance

Supersuck

The name says it all. Supersuck is one of Indonesia’s longest, most intense left-hand tube breaks, unfurling over a shallow, live coral reef. It only fires on solid swells, but when it does, you’ll see surfers link multiple barrels on a single wave. This one is strictly for advanced surfers with good tube technique.

Surf spot level:

  • Intermediate
  • Advance

Scar Reef

A powerful, world-class left-hander often compared to G-Land, Scar Reef throws big, walling barrels down a long reef and handles serious size. It’s the main attraction of West Sumbawa and a magnet for experienced surfers chasing hollow, high-consequence waves. Advanced riders only.

Surf spot level:

  • Advance

Yo-yos

A versatile break with both left and right options, Yoyos is one of Sumbawa’s rare quality right-handers and is best surfed at mid to high tide. Consistent and swell-hungry, it suits confident intermediate and advanced surfers, and it’s picked up enough of a reputation to feature on the competition radar.

Surf spot level:

  • Intermediate
  • Advanced

Tropical

Just around the headland from Yoyos, Tropical (Tropicals) is probably the softest wave in West Sumbawa, breaking beside a beautiful white-sand beach. It offers both lefts and rights and, depending on the wind, can be a fun, more forgiving alternative.

Surf spot level:

  • Beginner
  • Intermediate
  • Advanced

Eastern Spots

Lakey Peak

The crown jewel. Lakey Peak is a near-perfect A-frame that peels both left and right off a tight reef peak, with multiple barrel sections on dry-season swells. The left is the longer, more celebrated ride; the right is shorter, faster, and more technical. It’s world-class and expert territory, so know your limits before you paddle over.

Surf spot level:

  • Intermediate
  • Advance

Nunggas

Often called the main intermediate wave of the area, Nunggas (Nungas) is a long, workable left-hand point that peels with distance over raw power. The drop is easier than Lakey’s, making it a favourite for improvers and hotdogging surfers, while advanced riders enjoy it most in the dry season.

Surf spot level:

  • Beginner
  • Intermediate

Periscopes

A Sumbawa legend and a rare right-hander, Periscopes is a boat ride around the point north of Lakey. It offers a high, tight barrel over a relatively safe reef platform with a clean exit into the channel. It starts firing at higher tide and gets steep on bigger days

Surf spot level:

  • Intermediate
  • Advance

Maci Point

A right-hander breaking over a coral rock ledge, Maci Point works best on higher tide and picks up plenty of swell. It’s often a great call when Lakey is small, and trips out here can turn into a fun surfari-style day, sometimes with a bit of fishing along the way.

Surf spot level:

  • Intermediate
  • Advanced

Surf Spot Levels

Here’s a simple way to match Sumbawa’s waves to your ability. Remember that levels shift with swell and tide, so always check conditions on the day.

Beginner Spots

Beginners are best off in the sheltered Kertasari/Moro Bay area, where mellow, sandy-bottomed spots and gentle inside reefs offer friendly take-offs, where longboarders and first-timers usually start. Kertasari Bay’s gentle reef break at high tide is another kind option. Stick to high tide, go with a guide, and you’ll progress fast.

Intermediate Spots

Tropical, Yoyos (on smaller swells), Nunggas, and Periscopes reward confident intermediates with longer, faster rides and gentler sections than the heavy stuff. These are the breaks to work on your turns and get comfortable on reef.

Advanced Spots

This is where Sumbawa truly shines. Scar Reef, Supersuck, Lakey Peak, and Lakey Pipe deliver fast, hollow, powerful barrels over shallow reef for seasoned surfers. Bring your best boards, your booties, and your A-game.

How to Get to Surf Spots in Sumbawa

Once you’re on the island, most breaks are reached by scooter, car, or the occasional boat trip. Roads can be rural and unlit, so plan your travel for daylight. FAQ

Surfing in Lombok FAQ

Do I need to hire a surf guide in Sumbawa?

It’s highly recommended, especially for your first visit or for the heavier reef breaks. A good guide reads the tides and swell, knows which spot suits the day’s conditions, and keeps you safe over shallow coral. Even confident surfers save time and score better waves with local knowledge on their side.

Are there surfboard rentals available in Sumbawa?

Yes, many surf resorts, camps, and beachside shops rent boards, and some offer lessons for beginners. That said, Sumbawa is remote and selection can be limited, so if you ride a specific board or need a gun for bigger days, it’s smart to bring your own — plus backup fins, leashes, and wax.

How many days should I spend surfing in Sumbawa?

We’d suggest at least five to seven days to account for travel time and to give yourself a good window of swell and tides. A week lets you settle into the rhythm, explore multiple breaks, and enjoy a rest day or two without feeling rushed. Longer is even better if your schedule allows.

What should I pack for a surfing trip to Sumbawa?

Bring your boards and plenty of spares (leashes, fins, wax), reef booties for shallow sessions, a rash guard, and reef-safe sunscreen. Add a basic first-aid kit for coral scrapes, a dry bag, and light, quick-drying clothing. Since supplies are limited on the island, it pays to arrive well prepared.

Are there accommodations near Sumbawa's surf spots?

Absolutely. From simple surf camps and homestays to comfortable beachfront resorts, both coasts have places to stay close to the waves. On the west coast, being based near the Kertasari and Moro Bay breaks means you can check the surf before breakfast — which is exactly what makes staying with us so convenient.
