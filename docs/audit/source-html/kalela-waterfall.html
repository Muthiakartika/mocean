Kalela Waterfall, West Sumbawa

Tucked away in the green hills of West Sumbawa, Kalela Waterfall is one of those places that still feels like a secret.
Swim beneath a jungle cliff

Kalela's Hidden Tosca Pool

Kalela Waterfall rewards a short, scenic trek with something special: a tosca-blue pool glowing beneath a stepped, cave-like cliff. Wander past rice fields and forest, cool off under a gentle natural shower, then dry off by a giant vine-draped tree. This easygoing half-day escape ends with a refreshing dip and photos you won’t stop scrolling through.

What to See at Kalela

So what makes the trek worth it? Quite a lot, actually. Kalela packs several distinct experiences into one compact, magical spot.

Beautiful Cliff

Unlike a typical single-drop waterfall, Kalela tumbles down a stepped, holey rock face that almost looks like the mouth of a cave. The water discharge is gentle, which means you can stand right beneath it and let it fall over your shoulders like a natural shower. It’s a striking, sculptural sight and easily the most photographed feature here.

Small Pond With Its Tosca Water

At the base of the cliff waits the real showstopper: a small natural pond glowing in shades of turquoise and green. When the midday sun filters through the trees, the water lights up and practically invites you in. It’s shallow, calm, and refreshingly cool, very perfect reward after a warm walk, and a lovely spot to simply dip your feet and watch the little fish drift by.

Trekking Chance

For many visitors, the trek is half the fun. To reach the falls you’ll wander through farmland and forest along a rocky footpath, so there’s a satisfying sense of adventure and a little effort involved. The walk from the parking area is short (around 30 to 45 minutes) but the changing scenery makes it feel like a proper mini-expedition.

Big Tall Tree with Hanging Vine Roots

Keep an eye out for the enormous tree that stands guard beside the pool, its roots spilling and twisting down the cliff like something out of a storybook. Locals have even tied a rope to one of the big branches, so the more adventurous can swing out over the water. It’s one of the most memorable backdrops at Kalela and a guaranteed photo highlight.

Getting to Kalela from Sumbawa

Getting to Kalela is part of the journey. If you’re coming all the way from Sumbawa Besar, expect roughly a three-hour drive covering around 130 kilometres. From Taliwang, you’re much closer, at about a 30 to 45-minute drive to the village, followed by the short trek in.

Staying with us on the west coast puts you in the perfect position: from the Poto Tano and Tua Nanga area it’s an easy morning run over to Jereweh, and Kalela is only about 15 to 20 minutes from the well-loved Jelenga surf beach, so you can chase a waterfall and a sunset in the same day.

Nearby Attractions

Kalela pairs beautifully with the other gems scattered across this corner of the island. Here are a few worth stringing into your itinerary.

Agal Waterfall

If you enjoyed Kalela, Agal Waterfall is another must-visit destination in West Sumbawa. Surrounded by lush forest, this hidden waterfall offers a peaceful setting with fresh mountain air and a refreshing natural pool, making it perfect for a relaxing swim or a quiet break in nature. Visiting both waterfalls in one trip is a great way to experience the region’s untouched landscapes and scenic jungle trails.

Baru Lake

For a slower, more reflective stop, this wide freshwater lake near Taliwang is at its most beautiful at sunrise and sunset, when calm water mirrors the sky and fishermen glide past in wooden boats. Water lilies and reeds frame the shore, and herons pick their way through the shallows. Facilities are minimal, so come for the peaceful atmosphere rather than the amenities.

Poyung Beach

If you’re weaving toward the coast, this quiet local beach is a gentle detour, the kind of low-key, uncrowded shoreline West Sumbawa does so well. Expect soft sand, easygoing waves, and a laid-back village feel that’s ideal for a slow afternoon and an unhurried sunset.

Benete Beach

Set beside the busy Benete Port, this beach is a favourite for one delicious reason: seafood. Local eateries line the shoreline, so you can tuck into freshly grilled fish while watching boats drift in and out. The hills on either side make for a scenic backdrop, and the atmosphere turns especially lovely at dusk, when the harbour lights begin to sparkle.

Where to Stay Near Kalela

After a day of trekking and swimming, a comfortable bed near the coast is exactly what you’ll want. Here are three options to suit different styles of traveller.

Mocean Beach Resort

We may be a little biased, but there’s no cosier home base for your Kalela adventure than right here with us on the peaceful shores of Pantai Moro. Our handcrafted bungalows, beachfront restaurant, palm-lined pool, and yoga chalet all sit steps from the sea and our team loves helping guests plan trips to waterfalls, surf breaks, and hidden lakes. Come dive our house reef, chase uncrowded waves, and unwind at our  tropical beach resort, we’d love to host you.

Kalela is an easy morning trip from the resort, and we’ll arrange the driver and guide for you. Check room availability.

If you want to be closer to the surf and just minutes from Jelenga Bay, Myamo Beach Lodge is a charming boutique stay with an Australian beach-house feel. Wrapped in tropical gardens and set right on the sand, it’s a relaxed, surf-friendly option — and its Jereweh location keeps you close to Kalela.

Grand Royal Hotel

Prefer to stay in town with easy access to shops, markets, and restaurants? The Grand Royal in Taliwang is a straightforward, budget-friendly city hotel. It’s a practical launch pad for exploring the wider region if a central base suits your plans better than a beachfront one.

FAQ

Kalela Waterfall FAQ

How difficult is the trek to Kalela Waterfall?

It’s an easy-to-moderate walk suitable for most reasonably active visitors. From the parking area, you’ll trek around 30 to 45 minutes along a rocky path through farmland and forest. It’s not strenuous, but sturdy shoes with good grip make a real difference, especially if the ground is damp.

What is the best time to visit Kalela Waterfall?

The rainy season (roughly November to April) brings the fullest, most impressive flow, while the dry months can leave the falls gentler. Whatever the season, aim for mid-morning to early afternoon — that’s when the sun filters through the trees and lights up the tosca pool most beautifully.

Can I swim in the pond at Kalela Waterfall?

Yes! The natural pool below the falls is calm, shallow, and refreshingly cool, making it lovely for a dip. The gentle water flow even lets you stand beneath the cascade like a natural shower. Bring water shoes if you have them, and always take care on the slippery rocks.

Is Kalela Waterfall suitable for children and families?

It can be a wonderful family outing, as long as little ones are comfortable with a short walk and you keep a close eye on them around the water and rocks. Because facilities are basic, pack water, snacks, and spare dry clothes to keep everyone happy.

How far is Kalela Waterfall from Sumbawa Besar?

Kalela is roughly 130 kilometres from Sumbawa Besar, which works out to about a three-hour drive. If you’re staying on the west coast near Taliwang or the Poto Tano area, you’ll be far closer, often within an hour of the trailhead.
